<h2>Baku Race Moved Forward to Saturday</h2>
<p>This week, the F1 Grand Prix arrives in Baku, the capital of Azerbaijan. To avoid the national memorial day on 27 September, the entire schedule has been moved forward by one day, with the race taking place on Saturday 26 September over 51 laps.</p>
<h2>Russell Tops Both Thursday Practice Sessions</h2>
<p>Mercedes stood out on the opening day of practice. Russell topped both sessions, leading his teammate Antonelli by 0.552 seconds in the second session with a 1:43.347, while Verstappen was third. The two Ferrari drivers, Leclerc and Hamilton, were fourth and fifth.</p>
<h2>After Missing Three Rounds, Red Bull&rsquo;s Hadjar Finally Returns</h2>
<p>Hadjar, who missed the previous three rounds with a wrist injury, returns to the Red Bull cockpit this week. The team has also brought upgrades to the car&rsquo;s floor, diffuser and sidepods.</p>
<p>However, Hadjar hit a gearbox problem in the first session and lost track time; he completed more laps in the second session and finished ninth. Getting back up to race pace quickly is his key task this weekend.</p>
<h2>What to Watch in Baku</h2>
<p>The Baku circuit is roughly 6 kilometres per lap, combining long straights that favour slipstream overtaking with narrow sections through the old town. Cars must balance straight-line speed with cornering grip, and the setup trade-offs will shape the entire race.</p>
<p>The start is also worth watching: from the pole-position grid slot to the braking point for Turn 1 is just 111 metres, leaving drivers very little room to adjust. Once into the old town, the walls further shrink the margin for error. For the drivers, avoiding mistakes will matter as much as outright speed.</p>
