<p class="article-centered article-lead">Back on top in a wet Sepang: Verstappen takes his first win of the season</p>

Verstappen has finally taken his first Grand Prix victory of the year!

The Bahrain Grand Prix concluded at Sepang, with Verstappen crossing the line first, Antonelli second and Hamilton third. First pole of the season on Saturday, pole-to-win on Sunday — but anyone who watched the race knows this win was anything but easy. The start alone gave fans a scare.

Heavy rain, a delayed start, trouble on the formation lap — everyone filed back into the pit lane to wait some more. By the time the race finally got going, tire choices were all over the place: Red Bull and Mercedes on intermediates, Ferrari on softs, and McLaren on mediums. With the track close to drying, the teams had reached completely different judgements.

When the lights went out, Verstappen made a poor start: both Antonelli and Russell swept past, and Mercedes locked out the top two. The pole he had worked so hard for yesterday was gone just like that...

But the drivers who started on slicks soon paid the price, while Verstappen began fighting back on the intermediates. On lap 6 he latched onto Russell and completed the pass at Turn 4, leaving only Antonelli ahead. Then Bottas ran off the track, the safety car came out, and the front-runners all pitted. Mercedes went to mediums, Red Bull chose softs — once again, different calls.

The few corners after the restart were the most thrilling passage of the entire race. Verstappen went around the outside at Turn 1, Antonelli held on, and the two cars stayed side by side. It still wasn&rsquo;t settled at Turn 2, and Verstappen finally completed the move around the outside of Turn 3. The lead — taken back!

Antonelli had survived that first attack, but he couldn&rsquo;t force Verstappen to back out and wait for the next lap. The two cars fought from Turn 1 to Turn 3, and the Red Bull simply refused to go away. This battle deserves to be rewatched on its own.

With the lead regained, Verstappen gradually pulled clear. On lap 34 he and Antonelli pitted on the same lap, both switching to softs. Then on lap 43 Albon stopped, the VSC escalated into a full safety car, and the gap at the front was wiped out. Verstappen pitted, and Red Bull bolted on a used set of softs in 2.2 seconds.

While the safety car led the field around, Russell retired with a car failure — the cause still unclear. Norris, caught out by the slowing pack ahead, missed his braking point, ran off the track and tumbled to 11th. In this phase, even just following the train normally was costing people positions and points.

At the final restart, Verstappen held the lead and kept it to the flag, collecting a victory he had waited most of the season for. Behind him, Hamilton and Leclerc both passed Hadjar in turn — Ferrari was still recovering the positions lost at the start right up to the final laps.
