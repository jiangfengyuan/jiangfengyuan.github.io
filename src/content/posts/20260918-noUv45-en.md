<h2>Antonelli Wins Madrid&rsquo;s First Race, Extends F1 Points Lead to 81</h2>
<p>The first name in the winner&rsquo;s column at Madrid&rsquo;s new Madring circuit belongs to Kimi Antonelli.</p>
<p>In the Spanish Grand Prix on 13 September 2026, the Mercedes driver claimed his eighth win of the season. Max Verstappen finished second and Lando Norris third. After the race, Antonelli leads his teammate George Russell by 81 points in the Drivers&rsquo; Championship.</p>
<h2>Norris Controls the Start, Strategy Changes the Result</h2>
<p>Norris started from pole and controlled the pace through the first half of the race. Antonelli attacked in the opening corners but ran off the track twice and could not complete the pass. Norris then gradually pulled away, at one point leading by more than four seconds.</p>
<p>The turning point came when Stroll stopped with a brake failure. The virtual safety car was deployed, and Mercedes and Red Bull immediately pitted Antonelli, Russell and Verstappen. Norris missed that window and had to stop after the race resumed.</p>
<p>McLaren&rsquo;s tire change cost around seven seconds. When Norris rejoined, he was behind Antonelli, Verstappen and Leclerc.</p>
<p>Leclerc ran an extended first stint, and after his pit stop on lap 48 the top three positions gradually settled. Antonelli crossed the line first with a 4.351-second margin, Verstappen held on to second, and Norris could not make any further progress.</p>
<h2>Mercedes Takes the Inaugural Win at the New Circuit</h2>
<p>Antonelli&rsquo;s race was far from easy. His opening-lap mistakes briefly cost him position, and later in the race he reported an unusual feeling in the steering wheel. But Mercedes&rsquo; judgement during the virtual safety car earned him the most important thing of all: track position.</p>
<p>Hamilton&rsquo;s race ended early. He had moved forward at the start, but brake problems quickly worsened and he retired after just seven laps. Stroll also retired with brake failure, while Sainz and Perez failed to finish as well.</p>
<p>After the race, Antonelli said Norris had been quick enough to win on the day. The result, however, proved once again that F1 is not decided by qualifying or long-run pace alone. The timing of a safety car, the decisiveness of a pit call, and whether the pit crew delivers a clean stop can all reshape the podium.</p>
<p>Next, F1 heads to Baku from 24 to 26 September for the Azerbaijan Grand Prix. The Baku street circuit has a high accident rate and frequent safety cars, so the championship picture may well keep shifting.</p>
