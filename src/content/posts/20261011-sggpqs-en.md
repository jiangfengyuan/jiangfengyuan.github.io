**Hamilton was fastest in two sessions, but Verstappen took the Singapore pole!**

**Qualifying** for the 2026 F1 Singapore Grand Prix has concluded, with Verstappen taking pole on a 1:31.373. Leclerc was just 0.054 seconds behind in second, Hamilton third, Antonelli fourth.

**This is Verstappen's first-ever pole at the Singapore Grand Prix. But Ferrari were genuinely quick tonight — especially Hamilton in the first two sessions.**

![](/assets/uploads/MaxSG-Pole.jpeg)

Right from the start of Q1, the track was lively. Ocon locked up and ran off, narrowly avoiding the wall, and Stroll soon made a mistake of his own.

Hadjar had even worse luck. He had already retired from the sprint with a mechanical failure, and in qualifying the car lost power again. He never set a valid lap time — on one side Red Bull had just taken pole, on the other there was still a car to fix.

Hamilton set a 1:32.429 in Q1 to go fastest. Alonso, Albon, Stroll, Perez, Bottas and Hadjar were eliminated — neither Aston Martin made it through.



In Q2, Hamilton was fastest again.

A 1:31.529, with Max right behind him. At that point, Hamilton genuinely looked in contention for pole. Both Ferraris reached Q3, and Racing Bulls got Lawson and Lindblad through as well.

Hulkenberg just missed the top ten and was knocked out in Q2 along with Bearman, Ocon, Sainz, Bortoleto and Colapinto. Colapinto also brushed the wall on his final lap.



In Q3, Verstappen was first to set a 1:31.373.

There was one more run for everyone, but nobody could beat it. Leclerc improved on his final attempt to close to second, just 0.054 seconds off. Hamilton couldn't find any more time — fastest in both earlier sessions, he ended up third, which is a bit of a shame.

Antonelli took fourth, Norris fifth, Russell sixth, followed by Piastri, Gasly, Lawson and Lindblad.

![](/assets/uploads/SGGPline-up.jpeg)

(Race starting grid)

Russell won't be keeping that sixth place tomorrow. He has been handed a cumulative 40-place grid penalty for exceeding power-unit component allocations and will start from the back. Taking a penalty like that in Singapore makes recovering points a tall order.



Verstappen has already won the sprint and now has pole for the Grand Prix. But there's one thing he has to get right tomorrow: **the start**.

In today's sprint, Russell passed him off the line. Only when Russell crashed on the opening lap did Verstappen retake the lead. Tomorrow he'll have Leclerc alongside and Hamilton behind — he can't count on being handed a chance like that again.

With the two Ferraris second and third, at least they won't have to chase the Red Bull through a train of cars.

Hamilton was second in today's sprint and Leclerc third, and now both are in the top three in qualifying — a solid weekend so far. If they can stay with Verstappen tomorrow, they'll have options at the pit stops: one car can stop early to attempt an undercut while the other stays out. But first they have to keep the gap tight, otherwise even two cars won't trouble the Red Bull.

McLaren, meanwhile, just paid their tuition fee. On the final lap of the sprint, their two cars collided while chasing Leclerc — Piastri retired and Norris only salvaged eighth. The stewards added no further penalty, but the lost points aren't coming back.



Both cars are still in the top ten tomorrow; how far they can climb will depend on the traffic after the stops. Singapore is often a race where the car ahead is right in front of you and you still can't get past.

Pirelli still leans towards a one-stop strategy, with mediums and hards expected to be the main choices, though the soft may see more use this year. But when the safety car appears and which car you rejoin behind all have to be calculated on the fly. Russell, starting from the back, especially needs a well-timed window.



**The Singapore Grand Prix starts at 8pm Beijing time on Sunday 11 October, over 62 laps.**
