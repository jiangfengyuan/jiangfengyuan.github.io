Max Verstappen won the Singapore Grand Prix on 11 October from pole position, finishing 7.335 seconds clear of Charles Leclerc. Kimi Antonelli took third, 24.573 seconds behind the winner, after the late battle for the podium in round 17 of the 2026 season.<sup id="cite-1-1"><a href="#user-content-source-1" aria-label="View source 1">[1]</a></sup>

Verstappen had never won in Singapore. He put that right on his 250th Grand Prix start with career win number 73. Starting from his first Grand Prix pole at Marina Bay, he took his second win of 2026, a week after his first at Sepang. Red Bull now has back-to-back victories.<sup id="cite-2-1"><a href="#user-content-source-2" aria-label="View source 2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite-11-1"><a href="#user-content-source-11" aria-label="View source 11">[11]</a></sup><sup id="cite-12-1"><a href="#user-content-source-12" aria-label="View source 12">[12]</a></sup>

## What Antonelli needs to clinch the title in Austin

Antonelli started fourth and finished third. Mercedes team-mate George Russell’s chase was derailed: he crashed out of Saturday’s Sprint, then had to recover from 19th to ninth in the race following grid penalties for additional power unit components. That left Antonelli on 340 points and Russell on 238: a gap of 102 with six rounds remaining.

Antonelli can clinch the title at the next race, but winning alone will not be enough. All three of these must happen in Austin:<sup id="cite-6-1"><a href="#user-content-source-6" aria-label="View source 6">[6]</a></sup>

- Antonelli wins the race
- Russell finishes ninth or lower
- Lewis Hamilton finishes fifth or lower

Hamilton remains third in the standings on 229 points. Verstappen has moved ahead of Leclerc into fourth, with 221 to the Ferrari driver’s 215. The United States Grand Prix weekend runs from 23 to 25 October, with Antonelli’s first chance to clinch coming in Sunday’s race on the 25th.<sup id="cite-2-2"><a href="#user-content-source-2" aria-label="View source 2">[2]</a></sup><sup id="cite-9-1"><a href="#user-content-source-9" aria-label="View source 9">[9]</a></sup>

## Hamilton’s 44 laps on softs

Leclerc converted second on the grid into second at the finish. Hamilton, who started third, ended up sixth. When Lance Stroll’s breakdown brought out the Virtual Safety Car on lap 39, Leclerc stopped for fresh soft tyres. Hamilton stayed out.

Hamilton had planned a longer opening stint on mediums before switching to softs for a one-stop race. The mediums did not perform as he had hoped, and he stopped after completing 18 laps. Ferrari called him in during the Virtual Safety Car, but he missed the pit entry and then chose to continue. Forty-four laps on the same set of softs.<sup id="cite-4-1"><a href="#user-content-source-4" aria-label="View source 4">[4]</a></sup><sup id="cite-5-1"><a href="#user-content-source-5" aria-label="View source 5">[5]</a></sup><sup id="cite-10-1"><a href="#user-content-source-10" aria-label="View source 10">[10]</a></sup>

That left him defending against cars on fresher tyres. Leclerc passed for second on lap 46; Antonelli and Lando Norris came through in the closing stages. Oscar Piastri followed near the finish. By the flag, Hamilton had dropped to sixth.<sup id="cite-2-3"><a href="#user-content-source-2" aria-label="View source 2">[2]</a></sup>

He apologised over the radio and accepted responsibility for the decision and the points lost. Afterwards, feeling unwell, he was assessed by the FIA medical team and excused from the media pen. Ferrari released his post-race statement.<sup id="cite-4-2"><a href="#user-content-source-4" aria-label="View source 4">[4]</a></sup>

## The rain-hit Sprint and McLaren’s weekend

Heavy rain delayed the start of Saturday’s Sprint by around half an hour. Russell initially led but hit the wall at the end of the opening lap, allowing Verstappen back into first. Hamilton, who started sixth, rose to third at the start, inherited second when Russell crashed and held it to the flag. Leclerc completed the top three behind Verstappen and Hamilton.<sup id="cite-7-1"><a href="#user-content-source-7" aria-label="View source 7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite-11-2"><a href="#user-content-source-11" aria-label="View source 11">[11]</a></sup>

The McLaren drivers collided on the final lap. Piastri did not finish but was classified 14th; the final results show nine non-finishers, eight of them unclassified. It was a costly end to a disrupted afternoon.<sup id="cite-7-2"><a href="#user-content-source-7" aria-label="View source 7">[7]</a></sup><sup id="cite-11-3"><a href="#user-content-source-11" aria-label="View source 11">[11]</a></sup>

Both McLarens still scored on Sunday, Norris fourth and Piastri fifth. Liam Lawson was seventh, ahead of Audi’s Nico Hulkenberg, Russell and Isack Hadjar. Stroll, Franco Colapinto and Gabriel Bortoleto retired without being classified. Alex Albon also retired late but had completed enough laps to be classified 18th.<sup id="cite-1-2"><a href="#user-content-source-1" aria-label="View source 1">[1]</a></sup>

## Lambiase joins the celebrations

For all Verstappen’s control on Sunday, communication was awkward. He could hear Red Bull, but the team struggled to understand his replies. Red Bull used pit boards and signals from his radio button to check which strategy he wanted. Afterwards, he joked that race engineer Gianpiero Lambiase had probably enjoyed not being able to hear him.

Lambiase joined him on the podium as Red Bull’s representative. He is due to join McLaren no later than 2028, and Red Bull has confirmed to Autosport that Tom Hart will serve as Verstappen’s race engineer in Austin, Brazil and Las Vegas, with Lambiase still attending as Head of Racing. No permanent handover date has been announced.<sup id="cite-3-1"><a href="#user-content-source-3" aria-label="View source 3">[3]</a></sup><sup id="cite-8-1"><a href="#user-content-source-8" aria-label="View source 8">[8]</a></sup>
