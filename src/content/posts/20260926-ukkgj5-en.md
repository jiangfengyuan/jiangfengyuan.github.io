<p>Qualifying for the F1 Azerbaijan Grand Prix concluded on the evening of 25 September (Beijing time). Russell took pole with a 1:42.526, while his Mercedes teammate Antonelli hit the Turn 1 wall in Q1, damaging his suspension, and will start 16th.</p>
<p>Antonelli still led Russell by 81 points before the weekend, so the title fight is far from reversed — but this mistake gives his teammate a chance to close in.</p>
<p>Beyond the track, the proposed race-distance change, Hadjar&rsquo;s contract renewal and Haas&rsquo;s driver decision also dominated this week&rsquo;s paddock conversation.</p>
<h2 class="article-centered article-blue">01 | Russell Takes Pole by 0.837s</h2>
<p>Russell beat second-placed Leclerc by 0.837 seconds in Q3, taking his fifth pole of the season. Piastri was just 0.001 seconds slower than Leclerc in third.</p>
<p>Slipstreams on Baku&rsquo;s long straight help lap times. Russell himself mentioned after the session that the tow from Hadjar gained him some time on the straights. Even so, his advantage stands out compared with the tiny gaps between the cars behind.</p>
<p>Hadjar, Norris and Hamilton line up fourth to sixth, with Gasly seventh, Verstappen eighth, and Sainz and Colapinto rounding out the top ten.</p>
<p>After his crash, Antonelli could no longer fight for a result. Starting 16th means he must chase in the race — seizing overtaking and pit-stop opportunities while avoiding another mistake.</p>

<h2 class="article-centered article-blue">02 | Race Distance to Shrink to 290 km, Pending Approval</h2>
<p>On 21 September, the F1 Commission met in London and agreed to shorten the standard Grand Prix distance from 305 km to 290 km from 2027, to accommodate next year&rsquo;s adjusted power-unit fuel-flow rules. According to the official explanation, most races will be shortened by two to three laps.</p>
<p>The Commission also agreed to scrap the three-hour total time limit that included suspension time, replacing it with a fixed cut-off time for the event; the two-hour limit on actual racing time remains unchanged. The change aims to give rain- or red-flag-affected races a better chance of completing their scheduled distance.</p>
<p>Both proposals still require World Motor Sport Council approval and have not yet taken effect.</p>
<h2 class="article-centered article-blue">03 | Hadjar Renews with Red Bull, Returns to Qualify Fourth</h2>
<p>Red Bull announced on 23 September that it has extended Hadjar&rsquo;s contract; he will continue alongside Verstappen in 2027.</p>
<p>Hadjar had missed three rounds with a wrist injury, and Baku is his comeback race. The team confirmed the renewal before the weekend began, and he responded with fourth in qualifying — four places ahead of his teammate Verstappen.</p>
<h2 class="article-centered article-blue">04 | Ocon a Free Agent Next Year; Haas Line-up Still Undecided</h2>
<p>Ocon confirmed in Baku that he will be a free agent in 2027. Haas team principal Ayao Komatsu then said the team has not yet decided whether to retain him, with a line-up announcement expected within weeks.</p>
<p>Komatsu explained that Ocon&rsquo;s contract options have set deadlines, and the team simply not having chosen yet does not mean both sides have decided to part ways.</p>
<p>According to the official F1 website, Bearman is expected to stay, and Haas has recently tested and evaluated several drivers. The final occupant of the second seat remains to be announced.</p>
<h2 class="article-centered article-blue">05 | Upgrades Across the Grid, but Both Audis Out in Q1</h2>
<p>McLaren brought new sidepods, engine cover, floor edge, diffuser and rear wing to Baku; Red Bull revised its floor and sidepods with related bodywork changes. Williams introduced a new floor and rear-end design, while Audi updated its front wing, nose, floor, bodywork and rear wing, among other parts.</p>
<p>Mercedes and Ferrari declared no upgrades for this round.</p>
<p>In qualifying, Sainz took ninth for Williams, while both Audi drivers were knocked out in Q1. A single qualifying session is not enough to judge an upgrade package; the teams still need the race to verify long-run pace and tire behaviour.</p>
<h2 class="article-centered article-blue">06 | Monaco Gets a Sprint; Several Drivers Object</h2>
<p>The 2027 calendar, published on 16 September, increases the number of sprint events from six to ten, with Monaco included for the first time. This week in Baku, several drivers responded to the arrangement.</p>
<p>Hamilton believes Monaco needs other solutions to improve its racing and that adding a sprint is not the answer. Norris said there are more suitable venues, and Verstappen once again pointed out how difficult overtaking is in Monaco.</p>
<p>A sprint weekend brings two qualifying sessions, but the extra short race is still constrained by the circuit itself. How to create more on-track action is the main question facing this decision.</p>
<h2 class="article-centered article-blue">07 | Alonso Renews Criticism of New Rules, Citing Energy Management</h2>
<p>Alonso continued his criticism of the 2026 regulations this week, arguing that the energy-management requirements have changed the driving experience at some classic circuits, and hoping future adjustments will let drivers push flat-out more often.</p>
<p>According to Motorsport.com, he also mentioned potential rule improvements when discussing his future. But Alonso has not announced retirement at the end of the season, and these remarks should not be read as a decision to leave F1.</p>
<h2 class="article-centered article-blue">08 | Lights Out at 19:00 Beijing Time Tonight</h2>
<p>This year&rsquo;s Azerbaijan Grand Prix has been moved to Saturday to avoid the local memorial day on 27 September — an arrangement announced long ago.</p>
<p><strong>The race starts at 15:00 local time in Baku on 26 September, 19:00 Beijing time, over 51 laps.</strong></p>
<p>Russell will try to convert pole into victory, with Leclerc and Piastri right behind. Antonelli, meanwhile, must fight through from the midfield and limit his points losses.</p>
