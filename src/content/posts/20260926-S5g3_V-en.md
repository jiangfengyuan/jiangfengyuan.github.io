<p class="article-lead">On the evening of 26 September 2026 (Beijing time), the F1 Azerbaijan Grand Prix concluded at the Baku City Circuit. Verstappen closed in relentlessly at the end but still came up <strong>0.196 seconds short</strong>, finishing second. Russell converted pole into victory, Hadjar took third, and both Red Bulls stood on the podium.</p>

<p>In the closing stages it was impossible not to stare at the gap between the top two. By lap 46, Verstappen was within about 0.6 seconds of Russell, the two of them trading fastest laps with no room to ease off. Russell defended the lead while Verstappen kept chasing, and even on the final lap the order never changed.</p>
<p>On that last lap, <strong>Rus and Ver were 0.196 seconds apart</strong> — the pass just never came. Eighteen points for second place, but looking at that gap, it still hurts. Because it was <strong>only 0.196 seconds!!!</strong></p>

<p>Verstappen&rsquo;s Baku began with a string of overtakes. Starting on softs, <strong>he first passed Hamilton</strong>, then on lap 3 he and Hadjar <strong>both got past Norris</strong>. The two Red Bulls then each <strong>overtook Leclerc</strong>, and Hadjar <strong>let Verstappen through into third.</strong></p>
<p>By then, Russell was already pulling away up front — on lap 13 he led second-placed Piastri by nearly seven seconds, with Verstappen still stuck behind a McLaren. On lap 30, <strong>Albon hit the wall</strong>, bringing out the safety car; many drivers pitted and the field bunched back up. At the lap-36 restart, Verstappen passed Piastri at Turn 1 to finally reach a position where he could chase Russell directly. Shortly after the restart, another incident brought the safety car out again.</p>
<p>Hadjar brought it home third, 10.704 seconds behind the winner. The two Red Bulls scored 33 points combined — <strong>just 2 points fewer than Mercedes, who took the win and fifth place this weekend</strong>.</p>
<p>The McLarens probably don&rsquo;t want to watch this replay (lol)</p>
<p>McLaren&rsquo;s start was actually good: Piastri <strong>passed Leclerc around the outside of Turn 1</strong>, Norris got past Hadjar, and the two cars <strong>briefly ran second and fourth</strong>. Piastri held second for a long stretch, while Norris was progressively passed by both Red Bulls and Hamilton. Then on lap 30 he missed his braking point at Turn 1, locked up, and dropped to tenth. (Sigh.)</p>
<p>Norris&rsquo;s race ended in the lap-36 incident.</p>
<p>Colapinto <strong>braked late down the inside at Turn 1 and made contact with Gasly</strong>; Gasly <strong>then collided with Norris on the outside. And Norris retired.</strong></p>
<p>Gasly and Colapinto also retired soon after; in the same restart there was contact between Lindblad and Lawson as well. When the race resumed again on lap 38, only Piastri remained for McLaren — and on lap 40 he made his own mistake at Turn 1, dropping straight to fifteenth. He recovered to fourteenth, still out of the points. From second and fourth after the start to one retirement and one P14 — that swing really was a bit <strong>much</strong>.</p>
<p>The two Ferraris took <strong>fourth and sixth</strong>. Leclerc lost second at the start and was then passed by both Red Bulls, only <strong>returning to fourth</strong> after Piastri&rsquo;s mistake. &ldquo;Leclerc&rdquo; finally settled back into place, 3.432 seconds behind Hadjar (third) and off the podium.</p>
<p>Hamilton passed Norris on lap 17, but behind him Antonelli was charging back through the field — past Lindblad, Ocon, Albon, Lawson and Bearman in sequence, up to ninth by lap 23, then past Hamilton on lap 43 to finish fifth. Ferrari collected 20 points with fourth and sixth; Hamilton remains third in the Drivers&rsquo; Championship on 199 points after the race, with Leclerc fifth on 179.</p>
<p>The remaining points went to Lindblad, Ocon, Bearman and Sainz. The two Haas cars finished eighth and ninth, while Sainz only passed Hulkenberg for tenth on lap 49.</p>
