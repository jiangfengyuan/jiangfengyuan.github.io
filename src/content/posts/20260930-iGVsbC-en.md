<p><strong class="article-warm">Today is Verstappen&rsquo;s 29th birthday.</strong></p>

This season has really not been kind to Max. Fifteen races, not a single win, 163 points, sixth in the standings. The last time he went a whole season winless was 2015 — he was 17 and had just stepped into F1. In between: four world championships and 71 Grand Prix victories.

A few things from this year are worth remembering.

After the summer break, he stood on the podium six times in eight races. In Baku, he qualified only eighth, charged through to second in the race, and finished 0.196 seconds behind the winner, Russell. On paper this car isn&rsquo;t good enough for second — he grinds it up there, race after race. The broadcast cameras barely show this kind of drive; most of the time he is lapping alone in the gaps, with nothing to watch. He drives like this every single race.

Last May, at a Nürburgring Endurance Series test day, the entry list included someone called &ldquo;Franz Hermann,&rdquo; driving a Ferrari 296 GT3 in &ldquo;Verstappen.com&rdquo; livery, with the same helmet — so nobody was really fooled. When later asked about the fake name, he said it just needed to sound &ldquo;as German as possible,&rdquo; and the upside of not being on the list was obvious: &ldquo;At least it&rsquo;s quiet at eight or nine in the morning.&rdquo; (lol)

This May, he raced the Nürburgring 24 Hours with his own team, car number 3. They led for nearly 20 hours. With three and a half hours to go, the driveshaft failed.

<strong class="article-warm">Late August, Zandvoort, the final Dutch Grand Prix: he announced his renewal through 2030.</strong> When a journalist asked whether he had ever thought about leaving, he said: &ldquo;I think I&rsquo;m probably closer to retiring than to changing teams.&rdquo; A week later, he finished eighth at that very circuit.

One more thing worth mentioning: Max&rsquo;s number this year. He didn&rsquo;t go back to 33, and he didn&rsquo;t take the champion&rsquo;s number 1 (since he didn&rsquo;t win last year&rsquo;s title anyway). He chose 3 — <strong class="article-warm"><em>for the simple reason that he likes 3 more</em></strong>. This is exactly the kind of thing where he genuinely has no second reason to give.

Max&rsquo;s daughter Lily was born last May, which makes her over a year old now.

<p><strong>Twenty-nine. Four world championships, 71 Grand Prix wins, a team name that has followed him for a decade, a team that still can&rsquo;t win, a few choices he has never explained, and a kid who has just learned to walk.</strong></p>

<p class="article-closing">Happy birthday, Max</p>
